## HIERARCHY OF AUTHORITY

When adapting an existing hook: exact hook execution overrides global rules. When they conflict, the hook wins.

| Element | Inherit from the reference hook |
|---|---|
| Emoji count and placement | Zero in the hook = zero in the carousel. The register table describes tendencies. The hook is the truth. |
| Punctuation marks (`..` `!!` `/`) | If the hook doesn't use it, don't introduce it. Each mark is inherited per its exact role and placement. |
| Emotional intensity | Subdued hook = subdued carousel, even if the product is dramatic. |
| CAPS frequency | Match exactly. Don't introduce CAPS the original doesn't use. |
| CTA hype level | Read the actual CTA, not the register table. Hook 8's CTA is restrained. Hook 2's is excited. |

## THE RULES

### RULE 1 — HOOK FORMULA: 5 STRUCTURES, PICK ONE

| Pattern | Examples | Hooks |
|---|---|---|
| Identity claim | "for those who feel deeply", "rare habits" | 1, 7 |
| Confrontational accusation | "the life you hate", "kill the old version of you", "lowkey hate your own guts" | 9, 11, 12, 16 |
| Curiosity paradox | "so calm it makes people nervous", "disgustingly educated on every topic" | 13, 17 |
| Quoted internal monologue | "I consume so much knowledge but it never feels like it becomes a part of me" | 8, 14 |
| Direct aspiration / how-to | "How to make time for everything", "5 underrated habits", "Bored of the same podcasts?" | 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 15, 18 |

Match the reference hook's formula. Do not switch.

### RULE 2 — CASE SYSTEM

All lowercase = intimate/confessional OR raw/urgent (hook 9 is confrontational and all-lowercase simultaneously).
Sentence case = confident, giving advice.
CAPS mid-sentence = spoken emphasis.

Match the reference hook throughout. Do not switch.

### RULE 3 — PUNCTUATION

**Periods.** Primary tool. Two words can be a sentence. Two ideas = two sentences.
**Commas.** Lists and sequences only. Never connect two thoughts.
**/** Breathing pause inside body copy. Not a bullet. "listen while cleaning / review on the treadmill."
**!! ??** Genuine sincerity, not hype. "why didn't other apps think of this??"
**".."** CTA openers only. "Ya'll.. so I started using Recite." Never in content slides.
**No em dashes.** Ever.

### RULE 4 — THE PARENTHETICAL

**Hook slide:** can match main text in weight — it closes the open loop.
**Content slide headline:** always shorter than main text. Does one of four things: examples list / self-answering objection / conspiratorial whisper / practical qualifier. Never explains the tip.
**Sub-copy:** can be longer. Same four jobs.

### RULE 5 — PERSON

Second person in content slides: "your brain", "you can't."
First person for testimony inside a tip and always in the CTA: "i use", "i started."
Third person sparingly — only when contrasting two types of behavior.

The second → first person shift at the CTA is structural and always present. It feels natural when the energy level of the first person matches the energy the second person built. It feels jarring when the first person arrives louder or more excited than the tips that came before it. The register does not change at the CTA — only the pronoun does.

### RULE 6 — LANGUAGE: PLAIN WORDS ONLY

No technical terms. No academic language. Text-message level. If the word belongs in a textbook or a marketing brief, replace it with what a person would actually say out loud.

### RULE 7 — PAIN-MIRROR LANGUAGE

Use the exact phrases the audience says privately. Not marketing language — their language.
Original audience examples: "emotional dumping ground", "numbing yourself", "feel less alone."
For a new product, Step 3 Layer B replaces these with the new audience's native phrases.
Test: would this phrase appear in a group chat between people with this exact problem?

### RULE 8 — CONSEQUENCE FRAMING

Tips describe what happens if you don't do it — not "this will help you." Loss aversion lands harder than a benefit promise. At least one slide per carousel uses consequence framing.

### RULE 9 — SPECIFICITY AS CREDIBILITY

Every tip has at least one specific detail. "Uniball Micro 0.5mm" not "a pen." "16 minutes" not "a short video." Vague is forgettable. Specific gets screenshotted.

### RULE 10 — GRAMMATICAL IMPERFECTION

One or two intentional imperfections per carousel: missing apostrophes, lowercase proper nouns, casual spelling. Signals real person, real voice.

### RULE 11 — EMOJI SYSTEM

15 emojis across all 18 carousels. Each has one job.

| Emoji | Job | Where |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ | Tip bullet | Content slides |
| 🎙️ | Before product name | CTA only |
| 😭 | Sincere overwhelm (positive) | CTA complaint moment |
| ❤️ | Paired sincerity | With 😭 |
| 🥹 | Quiet gratitude | CTA sign-off |
| ✨ | Discovery / revelation | Hook or CTA |
| 👀 | FOMO / look at this | CTA |
| 😅 | Self-aware humor | CTA |
| 🩷 | Soft warmth | Hook on emotional content |
| 🧠 | Intellectual | Hook on learning content |
| 🌿 | Nature / wellness | Hook frames |
| 🗨️ | "Here's what I think" | Podcast recommendation slides |
| 🧡 | Warmth in discovery CTAs | CTA, discovery register only |
| 🍌 | Playful absurdism | Playful/self-aware hooks |
| 🎵 | Music / audio | Audio-referencing slides |

**Ceiling:** no emoji outside this 15 ever appears.
**Usage:** only emojis present in the reference hook are permitted. Zero in the hook = zero in the carousel.
**No stacking within one sentence.**

### RULE 12 — CTA: EMOTIONAL CONTINUATION

The CTA is the final beat of the register the hook opened. Energy, tone, emoji, and length inherit from the register — not chosen freely.

When adapting an existing hook: CTA inherits from the reference exactly.
When writing a new hook: write the CTA fresh. Words come from you. Register decides the energy.

| Register | Energy | Body tone | Emoji |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery / excited | High. First-person reaction to one specific moment of use. | Amazement. Short, breathless sentences. | 😭❤️ / 🧡🥹 / ✨ |
| Quiet / credible | Low. Plain testimony. | One specific use case. No hype. | 🎙️ or none |
| Community / inclusive | Conversational. | Casual aside. Community invitation. | 👀 / ✨ |
| Confrontational / purposeful | Direct. No warmth. | Product buried deepest. | 🎙️ |
| Playful / self-aware | Self-deprecating. Brief. | Emoji is the punchline. | 😅 |

**The test:** read hook slide + CTA back-to-back. Same person, same mood, same energy. If the energy shifts between content slides and CTA — register broke.

**Placement:** final slide / embedded as ⭐ tip (no mode switch) / no CTA. Match whichever the reference hook uses.

### RULE 13 — COPY IDENTITY

⭐ bullet — 13 of 18 hooks. Never replace with dash, dot, or number. Hooks 1, 6, 14, 15, 18 use a different format in its place.

### RULE 14 — 5 PAIN POINTS

1. "I consume a lot but retain nothing" — hooks 7, 8, 10, 17, 18
2. "I'm overwhelmed / burned out / can't relax" — hooks 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 11, 13
3. "I feel like I'm living the wrong life" — hooks 9, 14, 16
4. "I want to be smarter / more interesting" — hooks 6, 10, 12, 17
5. "I want to feel less alone / more understood" — hooks 1, 8, 11, 15

Same person, all 5 problems simultaneously.

### RULE 15 — PRODUCT NAME: CTA ONLY

The product name never appears in content slides. Content slides give general advice that stands alone — tips the viewer can act on without the product. The product arrives only in the CTA as the natural answer to "what do I use to do all of this." When the product appears in tips, the tips become feature descriptions and the carousel becomes an ad.

### RULE 16 — THE ONE LAW

The creator sounds like a friend who figured something out. Never an advertiser. The moment the viewer thinks "this is an ad," the machine stops working.

## IF THE USER ASKS FOR A NEW HOOK (NOT FROM THE 18)

Built from Rules 1–16 only. The 18 hooks are reference, not skeleton.

**Pick register first.** Everything downstream follows from it. Each register must be earned:

| Register | When earned |
|---|---|
| Discovery / excited | The product genuinely surprised the creator. Reaction is amazement, not just satisfaction. |
| Quiet / credible | Results speak without hype. Restrained testimony is enough. |
| Community / inclusive | The pain is one the audience feels alone about. Hook says "me too." |
| Confrontational / purposeful | Viewer is doing something wrong and needs to be told directly. |
| Playful / self-aware | The product or use case is inherently absurd. Humor is the hook, not a softener. |

| Decision | Flows from register |
|---|---|
| Hook formula | Curiosity paradox → quiet/credible. Confrontational accusation → confrontational/purposeful. Quoted monologue → quiet/credible. Direct aspiration → community/inclusive by default; discovery/excited only if product earns amazement. |
| Case register | Lowercase = intimate or raw. Sentence case = confident. |
| CTA | Energy, tone, and emoji follow from the register (Rule 12). Write the opener fresh — don't use phrases from the 18 hooks. |

**Structure from the product:** slide count = distinct tips the product has. CTA placement = final slide / embedded as ⭐ tip / none. Sub-copy length = emotional weight of the tip.

**Reference the 18 hooks for how each register reads.** Study sentence rhythm, energy, word weight, and paragraph length from the examples below — then write fresh.

| Register | Reference hooks |
|---|---|
| Discovery / excited | 2, 18 |
| Quiet / credible | 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 11, 13, 14 |
| Community / inclusive | 1, 10, 15 |
| Confrontational / purposeful | 9, 16 |
| Playful / self-aware | 6, 12, 17 |

State the register at the top of output, then write immediately.

## ABSOLUTE VIOLATIONS

- Title case on tip headlines
- Constructed argument in body — state it, don't argue it
- Stacked emojis mid-sentence
- Feature list in CTA instead of one specific use case
- Parenthetical in content headline longer than the main text
- Fake testimonial or quote inside a content slide
- Em dash anywhere
- Product name as first word or announced headline in CTA
- Personal complaint missing from CTA opener
- Adding or removing slides the reference hook didn't
- Writing "I built [product name]"
- Product name appearing in any content slide
- Emoji not in the 15-emoji vocabulary
